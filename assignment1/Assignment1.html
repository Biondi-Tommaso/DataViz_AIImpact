2019–2025: what formal proofs are & the Lean build-up

From fragmented assistants to Mathlib4

A formal proof is a mathematical proof written in a strict formal language and verified line-by-line by a computer's trusted kernel, guaranteeing zero logical gaps. Between 2019 and 2025, AI-assisted theorem proving shifted from older, fragmented systems (Isabelle, Coq/Rocq, HOL Light) toward Lean and its unified mathematical library, Mathlib4.

2026: the current frontier

Lean 4 as the standard AI verification language

In 2026, powered by neural theorem provers and reasoning models (such as AlphaProof, DeepSeek-Prover, and Goedel-Prover), Lean captures the dominant share of AI-driven formal verification research—turning autoformalization from a niche sub-area into a core pillar of mathematical AI.

Chapter 4: validation and preprints

From arXiv to Peer-Reviewed

In fast-moving AI research, findings often appear on arXiv months or years before formal journal or conference publication. Using all OpenAlex locations records, a paper posted on arXiv and later published in a peer-reviewed venue remains counted in both—allowing an exact measurement of publication velocity without undercounting historical preprints.

Why is the ratio ~2× in 2019–2025? Because our OpenAlex corpus spans all of Mathematics & AI—including applied mathematics, numerical analysis, engineering math, and statistics—where a large share of authors submit directly to journals (Springer, Elsevier, IEEE, SIAM) without ever posting to arXiv. In 2026, however, recent arXiv preprints have not yet completed peer review, pulling the ratio downward toward parity.
